## One sentence

Turn a real React bug into a playable debugging challenge, a reproducible replay and a verified regression test.

## The problem

“It broke in my recording” is an investigation clue, not a reproducible bug. Developers still need the exact actions, response order and assertion that explain the failure and prove that the fix works.

## The working experience

“Can you make this app lose your note in three moves?” Play a real small app, choose when controlled API responses arrive and try to trigger its bug. Reveal the cause and minimal action sequence. Replay the same actions against the fixed app. Keep the regression test that catches the bug if it returns.

Five known cases cover stale state, optimistic rollback, out-of-order search, duplicate submission and effect cleanup. Stars, best moves and next-case progression make them a playable learning loop. Personal scores stay separate from verified browser evidence.

The package includes an 87-second narrated demo, full spoken transcript, chapter-summary captions and offline playable cartridges. The video edits actual browser captures and recorded evidence into a product story; it does not present a fresh model call or continuous screen recording.

The November 3 video predates final guarded-runtime verification. Its spoken gate status is historical; the accompanying proof page records the current result. The original narration and exact transcript are preserved, with that context explicitly displayed beside playback.

## Why Meta is central

Muse Spark Open reasons over buggy source and timestamped recording frames and proposes browser actions plus a candidate failure assertion. Its accepted actions actually drive the reproduction loop. The verifier, not the model, decides whether the bug reproduced, whether a shorter sequence still fails and whether the fixed app passes the unchanged assertion.

The known-note example used a real Muse proposal with four actions, minimized to three, then verified three buggy failures and three fixed passes. The exact exported test also failed buggy and passed fixed in Modal.

A separate first-party coupon app was frozen before model use and kept outside the gallery/planner examples. No action plan, hints or explanation were submitted. Muse proposed three actions, minimized to two; three buggy failures and three fixed passes followed, and the exact exported test independently failed buggy and passed fixed in two further sandboxes. This is one **pipeline-held-out first-party example**, not an external benchmark, arbitrary-repository support or a model-training holdout. Gallery plans remain authored and labeled.

Three repeats under one schedule establish repeatability for those cases, not broad agent reliability. Successful examples are not an aggregate success-rate claim.

## Architecture and scope

React/TypeScript frontend; Python/FastAPI orchestration; persistent SQLite jobs; structured action validation; bounded Playwright execution; controllable API scheduling; assertion-preserving minimization and content-hashed artifacts. Model calls run outside submitted-code sandboxes. Execution supports small explicitly authorized Starter v1 React apps, not arbitrary repositories or execution-time dependency installation.

The guarded VM runtime separately passed six real boundary controls, all five unchanged seed pairs at three buggy failures/three fixed passes each, and the identical exported regression failing buggy/passing fixed. All 38 VMs in the verified cohort have terminal evidence. The memory proof combines exact kernel memcg/leaf/victim attribution with local OOM counters for the 1 GiB workload bound inside a 2 GiB VM. Earlier inconclusive outer-memory and failed orchestration/metadata attempts are preserved. The [public runtime derivative](../proof/runtime-summary.json) records source hashes, scope and outcomes.
